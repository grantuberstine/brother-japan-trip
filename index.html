The shape of the trip

Three bases, one direction, no backtracking until the last night

8 nights · 3 Tokyo → 1 Hakone → 3 Kyoto → 1 Tokyo
Land Saturday, go hard, then slow down.
The route
Tokyo · Sat–Mon (3 nights) — neon, food, Shibuya Sky at sunset, teamLab, Shinjuku nights. Two of the three days are a weekend; Tokyo is built for it.
Hakone · Tue (1 night) — 35 minutes by bullet train from Tokyo. Ropeway over a volcano, pirate ship across Lake Ashi, the red torii in the water, a ryokan with hot-spring baths and a 10-course dinner. Best Mt. Fuji odds of the trip at sunrise.
Kyoto · Wed–Fri (3 nights) — bullet train straight from Hakone's station (Odawara). Fushimi Inari at dawn, the Higashiyama temple walk, Gion at night, the bamboo grove, Golden Pavilion, a Nara deer morning.
Tokyo · Sat (1 night) — train back Saturday evening so Sunday's 6 PM flight is a stroll from a hotel 15 minutes from Haneda.
Big suitcases get shipped Tokyo → Kyoto on Tuesday morning (¥2,500–3,000 a bag, arrives next day). You carry one overnight bag to Hakone. This is the single best logistics trick in Japan.
Real talk · fall color
Late October is early for autumn leaves in Tokyo and Kyoto. The 2026 forecasts put Kyoto's ginkgo around Nov 27 and maples around Dec 11 — so the famous Kyoto red-maple photos won't be happening on this trip.
What you do get: crisp, mostly clear weather (the best Fuji-visibility season is just starting), first color in Hakone's high spots, golden pampas-grass fields at Sengokuhara, and the beginning of Kyoto's temple night light-ups (Kōdai-ji from ~Oct 23). Crowds are a notch below mid-November.
If leaves are the priority: swap Day 3 for a Nikkō day trip — the Oku-Nikkō lakes and waterfalls peak mid-to-late October. Details in Day 3, Option B.
Weather · late Oct
Tokyo / Kyoto: highs ~65–70°F, lows ~52–57°F. Hakone runs ~10°F cooler, chilly at night. Sun sets early — about 4:55 PM in Tokyo, 5:05 PM in Kyoto — so plan outdoor things for mornings and lean into the night stuff.
A rainy day or two is normal. Typhoon season is basically over by late October, but not impossible — one more reason for travel insurance on a no-change fare.

Day by day

A menu, not a schedule — pick what fits, skip what doesn't. Grant's picks from his own trips are marked ★.

Thursday, Oct 22 → Friday, Oct 23 Flight not booked
Getting there
Thursday night
Leave for LAX by ~9:30 PM Thursday. The ticket says Friday, Oct 23 because departure is 12:40 AM — the easiest way to miss this flight is showing up Friday night.
Online check-in opens 24 hours before: Thursday, just after midnight. Do it then and pick seats if the fare allows.
Do Visit Japan Web (see Prep) before you leave — immigration becomes a QR scan.
On the plane
It's past midnight — sleep immediately, as long as you can. Skip the first meal if you have to.
Wake ~3 hours before landing. Eat, water, no booze. You land at 4:45 AM Saturday Tokyo time (12:45 PM Friday in LA).
Japan is 16 hours ahead of LA (PDT). The move is: stay awake until 9 PM local on Day 1 and you're fixed by Day 2.
Saturday, Oct 24 · Day 1
Tokyo · arrival
Flight
American LAX → HND · land 4:45 AM Saturday · 12 hr 5 min nonstop
Immigration at 5 AM is fast — nothing else is landing. Trains into the city start ~5:15–5:30 AM. Add a Suica card to Apple Wallet (iPhone: Wallet → + → Transit → Suica) or grab a Welcome Suica at the airport — tap on, tap off everything, including convenience stores.
5–9 AM · the hotel won't have a room till 3 PM
Option A (best): book the hotel for the night of Oct 23 too and check in at 6 AM. One extra night buys a shower, a 2-hour nap and a real Day 1.
Option B: soak at the Haneda Terminal 3 hot spring (Izumi Tenkū no Yu, on top of the Villa Fontaine Grand hotel, right in the terminal) — bath, shower, breakfast, then into the city around 8.
Option C: drop bags at the hotel front desk and go straight to Tsukiji. Every hotel holds bags before check-in.
Morning
★ Tsukiji Outer Market — sushi breakfast, tamago on a stick, wagyu skewers, strawberry daifuku. Stalls run ~7 AM–2 PM. Graze 6–8 stalls instead of one meal.
Hamarikyū Gardens (opens 9 AM, ¥300) — a 17th-century shogun's garden ringed by skyscrapers, teahouse on the pond. Easy on airplane legs, 10 min walk from Tsukiji.
Ginza is next door — Itoya (stationery heaven), Ginza Six, the Mitsukoshi basement food hall.
Afternoon
Check in at 3 PM. Nap — 90 minutes max, alarm set, curtains open after.
Evening
Shibuya Sky at sunset — sunset is ~4:55 PM, so book the 4:00–4:20 PM entry online (~¥2,700). Sunset slots sell out weeks ahead. Open-air rooftop over the whole city, Fuji on a clear day.
Shibuya Scramble Crossing on the way out — cross it, then watch it from the Starbucks or the Shibuya Station walkway.
Dinner, both ★ Grant picks, both walk-in: Gyukatsu Katsugyu (breaded beef cutlet you finish on a hot stone, Dogenzaka, 15–30 min line) or Katsudon-ya Zuichō (tiny katsudon counter, 30–45 min line, worth it).
Saturday-night Shibuya is loud and fun. In bed by 10. Tomorrow starts early.
Sleep
Tokyo hotel · Shibuya or Shinjuku base · nights of Oct 24, 25, 26 (see Where you sleep)
Sunday, Oct 25 · Day 2
Tokyo · old east side + teamLab
Morning
Sensō-ji by 7:30 AM — Tokyo's oldest temple (645 AD). Kaminarimon gate, the Nakamise street before the stalls open. The tour groups land at 10; you'll have it to yourselves.
Yanaka — one of the few old neighborhoods that survived the war. Yanaka Ginza shopping street, narrow lanes, cats. ★ Hagiso café (a restored 1955 wooden townhouse) for a late breakfast — Grant's favorite.
Lunch if you linger in Asakusa: Asakusa Imahan (130-year-old sukiyaki) or Sometarō (cook-your-own okonomiyaki).
Afternoon
teamLab Planets, Toyosu — book a ~2 PM slot now; Sundays sell out (~¥3,800–4,800). Barefoot through water rooms and mirrored infinity spaces. Wear shorts or pants you can roll up.
90 min–2 hrs inside. Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai next door is an Edo-style food street with a rooftop foot bath if you want a snack after.
Evening · Shinjuku
Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building — free observation deck on the 45th floor, open till ~10 PM. There's a projection-mapping show on the building's face most nights after dark; check times.
Omoide Yokochō ("Memory Lane") — smoky alley of 8-seat yakitori counters by the station. Cash. Point at what you want.
Golden Gai — 200 tiny bars, 6 seats each. Look for "no cover charge" signs on the door; order one drink per person, move on.
★ Ichiran Shinjuku (solo-booth tonkotsu ramen, 24 hours) if you want the easy classic on the way home.
Sleep
Same Tokyo hotel
Monday, Oct 26 · Day 3
Tokyo · pick your day
Option A · west Tokyo (the classic)
Meiji Jingū at opening (sunrise, ~6 AM) — a forest shrine in the middle of the city, the wall of sake barrels, gravel paths with nobody on them.
Harajuku + Omotesandō — Takeshita Street chaos, then the tree-lined boulevard. ★ Samoyed Café moffu if you want to pet a giant cloud of a dog (reserve online weeks ahead).
Lunch: Tonkatsu Maisen Aoyama (kuro-buta black pork, walk-in).
Afternoon: Roppongi — Mori Art Museum + Sky Deck (open Mondays), or Tokyo Tower at sunset with Zōjō-ji temple framed in front.
Dinner: ★ Shodai, Ebisu — Grant's must-have. ~14 counter seats, modern kappo, the signature wagyu beef udon with potato foam. Reserve ahead. Backup: Yakiniku Jambo Hannare (A5 wagyu you grill yourself; book ~1 month out).
Option B · Nikkō day trip (if fall color is the priority)
Tobu limited express from Asakusa ~7:30 AM, ~2 hours (book seats online). Oku-Nikkō: the Irohazaka hairpin road, Kegon Falls (100 m), Lake Chūzenji, Ryūzu Falls — this is the zone that peaks mid-to-late October.
Tōshō-gū on the way back — the shogun Ieyasu's over-the-top gilded mausoleum (¥1,600). Back in Tokyo ~7 PM.
Monday means far fewer people than the weekend crush, but buses up Irohazaka still jam on peak-leaf days — leave early. If you do this, move Shodai dinner to Day 1 or 2.
Tonight · pack for Hakone
One overnight bag each. Everything else gets shipped to Kyoto tomorrow morning.
Ask the front desk tonight about Yamato luggage forwarding ("takkyubin") so the morning is quick. They'll fill out the form; you pay ~¥2,500–3,000 per bag.
Sleep
Tokyo hotel · last night here
Tuesday, Oct 27 · Day 4
Tokyo → Hakone
Morning
Ship the big suitcases to the Kyoto hotel from the front desk (Yamato, ~¥2,500–3,000 per bag, arrives tomorrow; the hotel holds them).
Shinkansen Tokyo → Odawara, ~10 AM — Kodama or Hikari, 33–35 min. Book on the Smart-EX app (seats went on sale a month out — they're open now).
Grab an ekiben (train bento) in Tokyo Station's basement. At Odawara, buy the Hakone Free Pass (2-day, ~¥5,000 from Odawara) — covers the mountain railway, cable car, ropeway, pirate ship and buses.
Midday
Hakone Tozan Railway Odawara → Gōra — a switchback mountain train, ~55 min with a change at Hakone-Yumoto. Drop the overnight bag at the ryokan (check-in is usually 3 PM; they'll hold it).
Afternoon · the Hakone loop
Gōra → cable car up to Sōunzan → ★ Hakone Ropeway over Ōwakudani — gondola over an active volcanic valley, sulfur steam, Fuji from the cabin on a clear day. At the top: kuro-tamago, eggs boiled black in the hot springs ("each one adds 7 years").
Ropeway down to Tōgendai → ★ pirate ship across Lake Ashi (35 min, camp as it sounds, great) → Moto-Hakone: Hakone Shrine's red torii standing in the lake. Bus back to Gōra.
~4–5 hours total. Check Ropeway status in the morning — it closes when volcanic gas spikes (buses substitute). If you have a spare hour: Sengokuhara pampas grass fields turn gold in late October — bus from Tōgendai.
Evening
Onsen before dinner. Rinse at the washing stations first, no swimsuit, small towel stays out of the water. Yukata robe for dinner.
Kaiseki dinner at the ryokan — 10+ small seasonal courses, included. Say yes to everything once.
Tattoos: most public baths still ban them. Pick a ryokan with private or in-room baths if either of you has ink (see Hotels). Early night; set an alarm for a sunrise bath.
Sleep
Hakone ryokan · Gōra or Hakone-Yumoto · night of Oct 27 (see Where you sleep)
Wednesday, Oct 28 · Day 5
Hakone → Kyoto
Morning
Sunrise onsen. The clearest Fuji window of the day is sunrise to ~9 AM. If the mountain is out, drop everything and look.
Ryokan breakfast (included) — miso soup, grilled fish, rolled omelet, rice, pickles.
Hakone Open-Air Museum (opens 9, ¥2,000) — sculptures across a hillside, a Picasso pavilion, a hot-spring foot bath. 5-min walk from Gōra.
Or skip it and sleep in. This is the one lazy morning of the trip.
Transit
Tozan railway down to Odawara (~1 hr with the change) · Hikari Shinkansen Odawara → Kyoto ~12:30 PM, ~2 hr 10 min. (The fastest Nozomi trains don't stop at Odawara — Hikari is the one.)
Smart-EX again. The suitcases you shipped yesterday are waiting at the Kyoto hotel.
Afternoon
Check in ~3 PM. ★ Nishiki Market while there's daylight (closes ~6 PM) — five blocks of stalls: yuba, dashimaki tamago, wagyu skewers, soy-milk donuts. Graze instead of a sit-down dinner.
Evening
Pontochō lantern alley and the Kamo River at dusk (sunset ~5:05 PM). The most cinematic 10 minutes in Kyoto.
Dinner: Mishima-tei (150-year-old sukiyaki house in Sanjō; reserve) or Chao Chao Gyoza (12-seat counter, crispy-bottom gyoza, cheap, cash).
Gion at night — Hanami-kōji street. If you see a geiko or maiko, don't chase for photos; the ward now fines it on some private lanes.
Sleep
Kyoto hotel · Gion / Kawaramachi / Higashiyama base · nights of Oct 28, 29, 30 (see Where you sleep)
Thursday, Oct 29 · Day 6
Kyoto · the east side
Dawn
Fushimi Inari at 7 AM (free, open 24 hours) — the tunnel of 10,000 vermilion torii climbing the mountain. Empty at 7, a conga line by 10. Walk up to the Yotsutsuji viewpoint (~45 min) and turn around.
JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station, 2 stops, 5 min. Eat a conbini onigiri on the platform.
Late morning · the Higashiyama walk
Kiyomizu-dera (¥500) — the big wooden stage hanging over the hillside. Then downhill: Sannenzaka → Ninenzaka stone lanes → Yasaka Pagoda (Hōkan-ji) — the Kyoto street shot everyone has.
Snack crawl on the way: Maccha House (matcha soft serve), Totaro (warabimochi). Lunch: Yamamoto Menzou (handmade udon, there's a line) or Kyoto Tonkatsu Katsuda near Pontochō.
Afternoon
Yasaka Shrine → Maruyama Park → Chion-in's giant gate. Then rest at the hotel 3–4 PM — tonight runs late.
Sunset
★ K36 The Bar & Rooftop (top of Hotel Seiryu, directly across from Yasaka Pagoda) — Grant's pick for sunset cocktails in Kyoto. Reserve, or be there by 4:30.
Night
Kōdai-ji autumn illumination — lit bamboo grove, a mirrored pond, projection on the raked gravel. The 2026 run is listed as Oct 23 – Dec 13, ~¥600, till ~10 PM. It's a 5-min walk from K36.
Dinner after, in Gion: Gion Karyō (kaiseki in an old machiya house; reserve ~2 weeks out) or ★ Menbaka Fire Ramen (they pour flaming oil into your bowl; silly and good).
Confirm Kōdai-ji's dates on the temple's site before you go — the 2026 window was still provisional when this was written.
Sleep
Kyoto hotel
Friday, Oct 30 · Day 7
Kyoto · the west side
Early
Arashiyama Bamboo Grove by 8 AM (free). It's a 10-minute path — be there before the tour buses or it's a 10-minute path with 2,000 people on it.
Tenryū-ji garden (¥500, opens 8:30) backs straight onto the grove. Then Togetsukyō Bridge and the river.
JR Sagano Line from Kyoto Station to Saga-Arashiyama, 15 min. Optional: Iwatayama Monkey Park (steep 20-min climb, macaques + city view). Optional: the Sagano Romantic Train + Hozugawa boat down the gorge — Mom & Dad's favorite day — but the gorge is only starting to turn in late October. Book it for the ride, not the color.
Lunch
Yoshimura (soba with a bridge view) or Shōraian (yudofu — Buddhist tofu cuisine — in a house above the river; reserve).
Afternoon
★ Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion (¥500) — taxi or bus from Arashiyama, ~30 min. Gold leaf on a mirror pond; 45 minutes is plenty.
Ryōan-ji rock garden is a 15-min walk away if you want the quiet counterpoint.
Evening · pick one
teamLab Biovortex Kyoto — the biggest teamLab in Japan, 7-min walk from Kyoto Station. Book a slot; Grant did 4 PM and it was the right call.
Or: Nishiki Market if you missed it + ★ Yukichi Honpo (refined Kyoto chicken-broth ramen, Grant's Kyoto ramen pick) + one more Pontochō walk.
Pack tonight. Tomorrow is Nara in the morning and a train to Tokyo in the afternoon.
Sleep
Kyoto hotel · last night here
Saturday, Oct 31 · Day 8 · Halloween
Nara → Tokyo
Morning · Nara
Check out, leave bags at the hotel desk. Kintetsu express Kyoto → Kintetsu-Nara, ~35–45 min (~¥760, or ~¥1,300 for the reserved limited express).
Nara Park — 1,000+ bowing deer (¥200 for crackers; they will mob you). Tōdai-ji's Great Buddha (15 m of bronze, ¥800). Kasuga Taisha and its 3,000 lanterns.
3–4 hours is plenty. Catch the mochi-pounding show at Nakatanidō on the main street near the station.
Afternoon · back to Tokyo
Back in Kyoto by ~2:30, collect bags. Nozomi Shinkansen Kyoto → Tokyo ~3:30 PM, 2 hr 15 min (Smart-EX). Bags under 160 cm total go overhead — no need to ship for one night.
Arrive Tokyo ~5:45 PM. Yamanote/Keikyu or a taxi to the last hotel near Shinagawa / Takanawa.
Night · it's Halloween on a Saturday
Skip Shibuya. The ward bans street drinking in the station area 6 PM–5 AM, fences off the Hachikō statue, floods the crossing with police and literally asks people not to come. It's a crush, not a party.
Do instead: Tokyo Tower lit up + Zōjō-ji (close to the Shinagawa side), a farewell yakiniku dinner (Yakiniku Jambo if you got the booking, or Shabuzen Roppongi for wagyu shabu-shabu), or Shinjuku's Omoide Yokochō / Golden Gai if you still have gas.
Swap option: stay in Kansai tonight — Osaka's Dōtonbori on Halloween Saturday is the wilder scene, 15 min from Kyoto by Shinkansen — then take a 7–8 AM bullet train Sunday (Shin-Osaka → Tokyo 2.5 hrs, Tokyo Station → Haneda 30 min). It works, but it's a hungover travel morning before a 10-hour flight.
Sleep
Tokyo · last night · near Shinagawa / Takanawa, or at Haneda itself (see Where you sleep)
Sunday, Nov 1 · Day 9
Home
Morning
Slow one. Checkout by 11 (ask for late checkout when you arrive).
Last picks: Sengakuji (graves of the 47 rōnin, 10 min from Takanawa), Tsukiji if you missed it on Day 1, or Ginza's Mitsukoshi basement for edible gifts.
Tax-free at bigger stores with your passport, ¥5,000+. KitKats and snacks are fine to bring home; fresh fruit and meat are not (see Customs).
Transit
Keikyu line from Shinagawa to Haneda Terminal 3, ~15 min. Be at Haneda by 3 PM for the 6 PM international departure.
Terminal 3 has a good food floor (Edo Koji) and the Day 1 hot spring if you want one last soak before 10 hours in a seat.
Flight
American HND → LAX · 6:00 PM Nov 1 → 11:10 AM Nov 1 · 10 hr 10 min. You land before you took off.
Mobile Passport Control app at LAX skips the kiosk line. Sleep the second half of the flight to land in LA-morning mode.

Customs & entry

Visa, paperwork, and what you can bring back

Oct 24 – Nov 1 · 9 days
🇯🇵 Japan
Visa
Visa-free up to 90 days for US passport holders — no application needed
Passport must be valid for the length of the stay · 1 blank page
Entry form
Visit Japan Web — pre-fill the immigration card + customs declaration online
vjw-lp.digital.go.jp · do it Oct 20–21 · generates QR codes you scan at immigration and customs · saves 20–30 min at 5 AM
Cash declaration
Declare if carrying over ¥1,000,000 (~$6,500 USD)
Heads up
No COVID rules · no vaccination or testing
Some common US meds are restricted (anything with pseudoephedrine, e.g. Sudafed; Adderall is banned outright). Bring prescriptions in original bottles.
Cash still matters — small ramen shops, temples, Omoide Yokochō and taxis outside the city can be cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards.
Nov 1 · arrival home
🇺🇸 USA re-entry
Customs declaration
Use the Mobile Passport Control app to skip the kiosk line at LAX — download it before you leave Japan
Duty-free allowance
$800 USD per person of personal goods · alcohol 1 liter · gifts count toward the total
Don't bring back
Fresh fruit (no Japanese strawberries or melons) · meat products, including vacuum-packed wagyu and most jerky · live plants
Fine: packaged snacks, KitKats, tea, sake (within the 1 liter), knives in checked bags, ceramics

Local etiquette

Quick cultural cards · don't be the rude tourist

🇯🇵 Everywhere
Tokyo · Kyoto · Nara
Tipping
Don't tip. Ever. It confuses people — they may chase you down to return it.
Shoes
Off when entering the ryokan, temples, and some restaurants (look for the raised step and a row of shoes)
Slippers usually provided; the bathroom has its own slippers — switch back when leaving
Manners
No eating or drinking while walking — stand by the stall, finish, then move (Tsukiji and Nishiki enforce this)
No phone calls on trains · quiet voices in public · no trash cans anywhere, so carry your wrappers till a conbini
Bow lightly when greeting / thanking — a nod is fine for tourists
Never stick chopsticks upright in rice (funeral symbol) · don't pass food chopstick-to-chopstick
Escalators: stand left in Tokyo, stand right in Kyoto/Osaka. Yes, really.
Geisha & photos
Gion's private lanes now fine tourists for photographing geiko/maiko up close. Shoot from the main street, don't follow, don't touch.
Ask before photographing people at shops and counters. Temples: look for the no-photo signs inside halls.
♨️ Hakone
Onsen & ryokan
The bath
Wash thoroughly at the sit-down shower stations before getting in · the bath is for soaking, not cleaning
No swimsuits · the small towel comes with you but never goes in the water (fold it on your head like everyone else)
Tattoos are still banned at most shared baths — use a private or in-room bath, or ask the ryokan what they allow
Don't swim, don't splash, keep your voice down · 10–15 minutes in, step out, repeat
The room
Yukata (cotton robe) is worn left-over-right — right-over-left is for the dead · fine to wear it to dinner and around the ryokan
Don't step on the tatami in slippers · the futon gets laid out while you're at dinner
Dinner is at a set time (usually 6–7 PM) — tell them at check-in if you'll be late
🚄 Trains
Shinkansen & subways
Shinkansen
Reserved seats on Smart-EX · line up at the painted marks on the platform · the train stops for about 60 seconds, have your stuff ready
Bags under 160 cm go overhead or behind the last row · bigger needs a reserved oversized-bag seat (free, but limited)
Eating is normal on the Shinkansen (that's what ekiben are for) · not on subways
Sit on the right side (seats D/E) Tokyo → Odawara/Kyoto for Fuji out the window
Local trains
Tap Suica at the gate, tap out at the other end — it does the math
Rush hour is 7:30–9:30 AM and 5:30–7:30 PM · avoid the Yamanote and Tokyo subways with luggage then
Last trains run around midnight–12:30 AM; after that it's a taxi (GO app)

Watch-outs

Pre-flagged risks · plan around these

⚠Oct 22
The flight is "Friday" at 12:40 AM
That's Thursday night. Be at LAX by ~10 PM Thursday, Oct 22. Put it in the calendar as Thursday, not Friday.
⚠Fare
Basic Plus Bag = no changes, no refunds
If the dates aren't locked, the $260 Main Cabin upgrade is cheaper than a new ticket. Either way, buy travel insurance.
⚠Oct 24
4:45 AM arrival, no room until 3 PM
Decide Day 1's Option A / B / C before you go. Option A (book Oct 23 too) is the one that makes Day 1 actually good.
⚠Oct 24
Jet lag · 16-hour shift
Sleep on the plane right away. Day 1 nap ≤ 90 min. Daylight and walking. Bed at 9–10 PM local. Day 2 you'll wake at 5 AM naturally — perfect for Sensō-ji.
⚠All trip
It's not peak fall color
Kyoto maples peak mid-December in the 2026 forecast. Expect green with a few early reds in Tokyo/Kyoto, real color only up in Hakone's heights and Nikkō. Go for the light-ups, the weather and the empty-ish temples instead.
⚠Oct 27
Hakone Ropeway can close for volcanic gas
Check the Odakyu Hakone site that morning. When it's closed, buses run the same loop; you lose the gondola view but still get the lake, ship and torii.
⚠Oct 27
Luggage forwarding timing
Ship by ~10 AM Oct 27 → arrives at the Kyoto hotel Oct 28 (usually afternoon). If that makes you nervous, ship the evening of Oct 26 instead and it arrives a day early; the hotel will hold it. Keep the slip.
⚠Oct 27–28
Tattoos at the onsen
Most shared baths still say no. Book a ryokan with private/in-room baths or buy cover-up patches — don't find this out in the changing room.
⚠Oct 29
Kōdai-ji light-up dates were provisional
Listed Oct 23 – Dec 13 for 2026 by one source. Check kodaiji.com the week before. If it hasn't started, Yasaka Shrine's lanterns and Gion are lit anyway.
⚠Oct 31
Halloween · Shibuya is a no
Street-drinking ban 6 PM–5 AM, Hachikō fenced off, heavy police, the ward asks people to stay away. Don't book the last-night hotel in Shibuya either — it'll be loud till 2 AM.
⚠Oct 31
Saturday-afternoon Shinkansen seats
Kyoto → Tokyo on a Saturday afternoon fills up. Book the Oct 31 ~3:30 PM Nozomi now, not that week.
⚠Everywhere
Cash-only pockets
Omoide Yokochō, Golden Gai, temple entries, small ramen counters, Nara deer crackers. Keep ¥10,000 in small bills on you at all times.
